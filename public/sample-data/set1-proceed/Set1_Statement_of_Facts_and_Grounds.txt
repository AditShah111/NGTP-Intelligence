STATEMENT OF FACTS
========================================================================================================

1. The Appellant, M/s Apex Precision Engineering Pvt. Ltd., is a private limited company engaged in the manufacturing of precision automotive components and industrial assemblies, holding valid GST registration bearing GSTIN: 27AAACA9876C1Z3.

2. In the regular course of manufacturing business during Financial Year 2018-19, the Appellant procured 40.00 Metric Tonnes of Hot Rolled Prime Steel Coils (HSN Code 7208) from M/s Delta Metal Alloys Ltd., having its principal place of business at Plot No. 42, MIDC Taloja, Navi Mumbai (GSTIN: 27AABCD5544E1Z2).

3. AT THE TIME OF THE TRANSACTION (October 2018), the supplier M/s Delta Metal Alloys Ltd. held a VALID AND ACTIVE GST registration. The supplier's GSTIN was verified on the GST Common Portal before the transaction. The portal confirmed the supplier's status as "Active" and the supplier was filing regular monthly and quarterly returns.

4. The said procurement was covered under genuine Tax Invoice No. DMA/2018-19/0402 dated 12-10-2018 for a total value of INR 2,51,73,333/-, comprising Taxable Value of INR 2,13,33,333/-, CGST of INR 19,20,000/-, and SGST of INR 19,20,000/- (Total GST: INR 38,40,000/-). The invoice contained all mandatory particulars prescribed under Rule 46 of the CGST Rules, 2017.

5. The physical movement and transportation of the goods from Taloja, Navi Mumbai to the Appellant's manufacturing plant in Chakan, Pune was executed through Maharashtra Roadways Lines under Consignment Lorry Receipt No. MRL/PUN/8921 dated 12-10-2018 and valid E-Way Bill No. 241089201945 generated on 12-10-2018. The E-Way Bill was generated by the supplier using its ACTIVE GSTIN; the GST portal itself validated the registration at the time of E-Way Bill generation.

6. Physical delivery and weighment were contemporaneously recorded at Dharamnath Computerized Weighbridge under Slip No. DWB/18/10492 dated 13-10-2018 (Gross Weight: 58,420 Kg; Tare Weight: 18,380 Kg; Net Weight: 40,040 Kg matching the invoice quantity). The vehicle movement is further corroborated by electronic NHAI FASTag toll records across Khalapur, Kusgaon, and Talegaon toll plazas on the Mumbai-Pune Expressway on 12-10-2018.

7. The Appellant discharged 100% of the invoice consideration along with full GST amounting to INR 2,51,73,333/- to the registered bank account of the supplier M/s Delta Metal Alloys Ltd. (State Bank of India A/c No. 30198472911) through RTGS banking channels from its HDFC Bank Current Account No. 50200028914421 under UTR No. HDFCR52018102400918234 on 24-10-2018 (within 12 days of invoice date, fully compliant with the 180-day mandate under Second Proviso to Section 16(2)).

8. The supplier duly reported the transaction in its outward return Form GSTR-1 for October 2018 filed on 10-11-2018 under ARN: AA2710180918234, and the invoice duly populated in the Appellant's Form GSTR-2A. The Appellant availed the credit in its regular monthly Form GSTR-3B filed on 20-11-2018.

9. NEARLY 5 YEARS AFTER THE TRANSACTION, vide order dated 15-06-2023, the registration of M/s Delta Metal Alloys Ltd. was RETROSPECTIVELY CANCELLED with effect from 01-07-2017 under Section 29(2), pursuant to a DGGI investigation classifying the supplier as a Non-Genuine Taxpayer (NGTP). The Appellant was never informed of this cancellation, was never given opportunity of hearing before the cancellation, and had no role or involvement in any alleged default by the supplier.

10. Notwithstanding the undisputed documentary evidence proving genuine receipt of goods and payment of tax through banking channels, the Respondent passed the Impugned Order-in-Original in Form GST DRC-07 dated 18-03-2024 confirming disallowance of ITC of INR 38,40,000/-, demanding interest of INR 36,86,400/- under Section 50, and imposing 100% penalty of INR 38,40,000/- under Section 74(1), solely on the ground that the supplier's GSTIN was retrospectively cancelled.

11. Critically, in para 5 of the impugned order, the Respondent admits that NO recovery proceedings have been initiated against the supplier M/s Delta Metal Alloys Ltd. under Section 73/74, as the proprietor is stated to be "non-traceable."

12. Aggrieved by the Impugned Order, the Appellant prefers this Appeal before the Hon'ble Appellate Authority.

========================================================================================================
GROUNDS OF APPEAL
========================================================================================================

GROUND I: RETROSPECTIVE CANCELLATION CANNOT EXTINGUISH RIGHTS OF BONA FIDE BUYERS - D.Y. BEATHEL ENTERPRISES RATIO
For that the Adjudicating Authority erred in denying ITC to the Appellant solely on the basis of retrospective cancellation of the supplier's registration. The Hon'ble Madras High Court in D.Y. Beathel Enterprises v. State Tax Officer (WP No. 8253/2020) held that retrospective cancellation of a supplier's registration cannot prejudice an innocent buyer who transacted with the supplier when the registration was active on the government's own portal. A bona fide purchaser who verified the supplier's registration on the GST portal, received genuine goods, and paid tax through banking channels cannot be penalized for an administrative act of retrospective cancellation that occurred years after the transaction.

GROUND II: VIOLATION OF ARTICLE 141 PRECEDENT - SUNCRAFT ENERGY SUPREME COURT RATIO
For that the Adjudicating Authority erred in demanding tax from the Appellant under Section 16(2)(c) without first initiating any recovery proceedings against the selling dealer. In Suncraft Energy Pvt. Ltd. v. Assistant Commissioner of State Tax (MAT 1218 of 2023), the Hon'ble Calcutta High Court held that recovery proceedings cannot be initiated against the purchasing recipient without first exhausting all statutory avenues against the defaulting supplier. The Hon'ble Supreme Court affirmed this principle on 14-12-2023 by dismissing the Revenue's Special Leave Petition in SLP (C) No. 27927/2023. Under Article 141 of the Constitution of India, this ruling is binding. In para 5 of the impugned order, the Respondent admits that no recovery was initiated against the seller.

GROUND III: VIOLATION OF DOCTRINE OF IMPOSSIBILITY (LEX NON COGIT AD IMPOSSIBILIA) & ARTICLE 14
For that the Respondent erred in imposing an impossible condition on the Appellant. No statute or rule empowers a buying entity to prevent retrospective cancellation of a supplier's GSTIN. The Appellant exercised all due diligence within its control: verified GSTIN on the portal, obtained valid tax invoice, took physical delivery, and paid full consideration via RTGS. The Supreme Court in Arise India Ltd. v. CTT held that the law cannot compel a taxpayer to perform that which is physically and legally impossible.

GROUND IV: ESTOPPEL AGAINST THE GOVERNMENT - PORTAL VALIDATED THE SUPPLIER AT EVERY STEP
For that the Government's own GST Common Portal accepted the supplier's GSTR-1 filing, auto-populated the invoice into GSTR-2A, and permitted E-Way Bill generation using the supplier's GSTIN. The Government cannot subsequently turn around and deny credit to a buyer who relied on the Government's own portal validation. The doctrine of promissory estoppel and legitimate expectation applies.

GROUND V: PROSPECTIVE APPLICABILITY OF SECTION 16(2)(aa) & CIRCULAR 183 SAFE-HARBOR RELIEF
For that the Respondent erred in retrospectively applying GSTR-2B matching restrictions to FY 2018-19. Section 16(2)(aa) was inserted prospectively w.e.f. 01.01.2022 (M. Trade Links v. Union of India, Kerala HC DB). CBIC Circular No. 183/15/2022-GST provides binding safe-harbor protection where the supplier reported the invoice in GSTR-1 and tax was paid through banking channels.

GROUND VI: REBUTTAL OF PAPER-ONLY ALLEGATION THROUGH UNBROKEN PHYSICAL TRANSIT CHAIN
For that the Respondent erred in questioning the genuineness of the supply without rebutting the contemporaneous physical movement records (E-Way Bill Part A & B, Weighbridge Slip, FASTag toll receipts). In M/s Halder Enterprises v. State of West Bengal (WPA 23512 of 2023), the Calcutta High Court held that where transactions are supported by genuine E-Way bills, vehicle toll entries, and weighbridge records, allegations of paper-only transactions cannot sustain.

GROUND VII: ILLEGALITY OF SECTION 74 EXTENDED PERIOD & PENALTY IN ABSENCE OF MENS REA
For that the Respondent erred in invoking Section 74(1) and imposing 100% penalty. The Appellant recorded all transactions in audited books and regular GSTR-3B returns. There is zero evidence of collusion between the Appellant and the supplier. The retrospective cancellation of the supplier is an administrative action against the supplier, not evidence of fraud by the buyer. The Supreme Court in Uniworth Textiles Ltd. v. CCE settled that penalty cannot be imposed without proving deliberate deception against the specific person charged.

PRAYER
Wherefore, the Appellant respectfully prays that the Hon'ble Appellate Authority may be pleased to:
(a) Quash and set aside the Impugned Order-in-Original dated 18-03-2024 passed solely on the ground of retrospective cancellation of the supplier's GSTIN;
(b) Restore the Input Tax Credit of INR 38,40,000/- claimed by the Appellant;
(c) Hold that retrospective cancellation of a supplier's registration cannot extinguish ITC rights of a bona fide buyer who transacted during the period of valid registration;
(d) Drop all demands of interest under Section 50 and penalty under Section 74;
(e) Pass such other or further order(s) as this Hon'ble Authority may deem fit and proper.
